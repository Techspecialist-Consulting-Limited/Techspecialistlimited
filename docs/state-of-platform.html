Executive Briefing AI Recruitment Platform 17 August 2026

State of the Platform

What the system does today, what it costs to run, where it is exposed, and the decisions required to either keep operating it safely or take it to market.

Hiring pipeline Operational Complete and in daily use, from job posting through to hire.
Security posture Three items blocking Two credential exposures and one gap in the decision record.
Capacity Constrained Two known bottlenecks surface under applicant surges.
Sellable as a product Not yet Four structural gaps, one of which gets costlier the longer it waits.

The platform works. It runs the full hiring process end to end, and the AI-conducted voice interview is genuinely ahead of what most commercial vendors offer. The core asset is real.

It is not currently safe to leave as it is. An administrative panel holding captured personal data appears to have no access control, production credentials can silently fall back to values published in the source, and the system records what decisions were made about candidates but not who made them. These are days of work, not months, and they should be closed before anything else is considered.

Selling it is credible but requires a deliberate decision now. The system was built for one company and has no concept of separate customers anywhere in its data. That is the single most expensive thing to change later, and the cost rises with every month of further development.

How decisions are actually made

The AI scores, transcribes, summarises and prioritises. It does not decide. Advancing a candidate can happen automatically when they clear the pass mark, but no candidate is ever rejected, hired or removed without a member of staff signing in and taking that action. This is the operating model regulators and enterprise buyers expect, and the platform already follows it.

Section 02

Decisions required now

Ranked by exposure. Effort figures are engineering estimates for the change itself, not including review and release.

Blocking Days

An administrative panel holding personal data has no access control

The dashboard holding marketing leads captured from the public AI readiness quiz, which includes names, email addresses and company names, appears to have no login at any layer. Anyone who finds the address could view, export or permanently delete that data. Evidence in the codebase suggests protection was started and never finished.

Decision: authorise immediate verification against the live environment, and closure the same week if confirmed. This is the highest exposure item on the list and among the cheapest to fix.

Blocking Days

Production credentials can silently fall back to published defaults

The system's API key, session signing secret and HR password all have working default values written into the source code, and the system's development mode is on by default. A correct deployment replaces them, but nothing stops the system starting if a setting is missing. A single dropped configuration value during a deployment would put production onto a publicly known password without any warning.

Decision: confirm current production values, then require the system to refuse to start rather than fall back to a default.

Blocking Days

The record shows what was decided, but not who decided it

Every action taken on a candidate is logged with a timestamp and a reason. The field for the member of staff responsible exists and is displayed in the interface, but is never filled in. In practice the platform can show that a candidate was rejected and when, and cannot show who authorised it.

Decision: approve the fix. This is what turns an activity log into a defensible decision record, and it is the difference between being able and unable to answer a candidate challenge or a regulator. Everything needed for it is already built.

High 1 to 2 weeks

Candidate invitations can be lost without trace

When automatic shortlisting is switched on, the interview invitation is held inside the running web process for a short delay before sending. A restart or redeployment during that window loses the invitation permanently, with no retry and no record. Given that deployments here are already known to sometimes need repeating, this will happen.

Decision: approve moving this onto durable infrastructure. A qualified candidate silently never being contacted is both a hiring loss and a reputational one.

High 1 to 2 weeks

The application form is the first thing to break under volume

CV screening runs while the candidate waits on the submission page rather than in the background. Each applicant holds a connection open for the length of an AI call. This is the constraint that fails during a hiring surge, which is precisely the situation automatic shortlisting exists to handle.

Decision: approve alongside the item above. They are the same piece of work and should be done together.

High 1 to 2 weeks

Every HR account can do everything

There is no administrator, recruiter or read-only distinction. Any account can permanently delete a candidate or clear every application on a job. The risk is accidental loss as much as misuse, and it grows with each person given access.

Decision: approve basic role tiers before the team using the system grows further. Adding permissions after the fact is materially harder.

Medium Weeks

Candidates below the pass mark wait indefinitely

Because nobody is rejected automatically, candidates who do not clear the mark remain open with no target response time, no automatic communication and nothing prompting HR to act. Silence after applying is the most damaging failure in candidate experience and the one most likely to reach social media.

Decision: set an internal response-time commitment, and approve a queue that shows HR how long people have been waiting.

Medium Weeks

Candidate data is kept indefinitely

CVs, cover letters and full interview transcripts are retained with no expiry and no automatic deletion. Common guidance is to hold unsuccessful applicants' data for no more than six to twelve months unless they have agreed to stay on file.

Decision: set the retention period as policy, then approve building the control that enforces it.

Policy Months of lead time

Fairness cannot currently be tested

The platform collects no demographic information of any kind. That is a reasonable privacy position, and it also means there is no way to check whether AI scoring produces different outcomes across groups. Independent bias audits are becoming a purchasing requirement, and larger buyers increasingly ask for one before agreeing to a demo.

Decision: decide whether to begin collecting voluntary, separated self-identification data purely for fairness monitoring. This is a policy and consent question, not a technical one, and it has the longest lead time of anything on this list. Nothing else about a bias audit can start until it is answered.

Low Days

Two candidate-facing refinements

The AI disclosure shown before applying should name the specific criteria assessed, such as years of experience, listed skills and education level, rather than stating only that AI is used. Separately, rejection emails could carry one or two specific lines drawn from evidence the system already captures, which very few employers do at all. The interview lockout should also move from ending the session on the first loss of window focus to flagging repeated occurrences for review, since the current trigger also fires on ordinary desktop interruptions and assistive technology.

Decision: approve as a batch. These are copy and configuration changes with a disproportionate effect on how candidates perceive the process.

Section 03

What it costs to run

The voice interview is the dominant cost per candidate. Live speech-to-speech AI is billed by the minute and is budgeted at twenty minutes per interview by default, with a forty-five minute ceiling. CV screening, email and hosting are minor by comparison.

That cost scales with the number of candidates advanced, not the number who apply. This matters directly: automatic shortlisting exists to move more people forward with less HR effort, so raising the pass mark lowers cost and lowering it raises cost. The pass mark is therefore a budget control as well as a quality one, and should be set with both in mind.

One operational check is outstanding. The live voice interview is governed by a switch that is off unless explicitly enabled. Its state in the production environment should be confirmed, since the answer determines whether the most expensive and most differentiating feature is actually running.

Section 04

Taking it to market

The AI-native screening and interviewing core is a genuine differentiator and would stand up against established vendors. Four structural gaps sit between the current system and something that can be sold.

  • It has no concept of separate customers. The company name, brand colour and logo are settings, and nothing in the underlying data separates one organisation's candidates from another's. Introducing that later touches every part of the system. This is the largest and least reversible item, and the only one that gets more expensive the longer normal development continues. It warrants a decision now even if the answer is to defer, because the answer changes how the system should be built in the meantime.
  • Corporate sign-in is absent. Business buyers expect staff to log in with their existing company accounts and to have access removed automatically when someone leaves. The platform has its own login only.
  • Access tiers and retention controls are procurement checklist items. Both already appear above as operational needs. They are listed again here because their absence will surface in any serious purchasing review.
  • A current bias audit is increasingly the price of entry. Gated on the demographic data decision in the previous section, which is why that question has the longest lead time despite looking like the smallest item.
Section 05

Recommended sequence

01

Close the two credential exposures and record who acts

The three blocking items together. Days of work, and they remove the exposures that would be hardest to explain if something went wrong first.

02

Make the pipeline durable

Move screening and delayed invitations onto infrastructure that survives a restart. Removes the lost-candidate failure and the volume bottleneck in one piece of work.

03

Add role tiers

Before the number of people with access grows further.

04

Fix the candidate experience batch

Waiting-time queue and response commitment, specific disclosure wording, feedback in rejection emails, and less trigger-happy interview lockout.

05

Set and enforce retention

Policy decision first, then the control that applies it automatically.

06

Answer the two strategic questions

Whether to collect fairness-monitoring data, and whether this is being built to sell. Both need answering before further significant development, because both change what should be built next.
